The three problems

1 · The shared parser reads dates month first · #1

This problem holds 374 reports. Every tenant on file writes the day first, and the shared parser reads every date month first, so day and month swap: “4 March turned into 3 April”. It hits every tenant, through the app, by email and through the booking-v2 form. This is the problem #4471 describes.

2 · The booking-v3 form hints MM/DD · #2

This problem holds 33 reports, all sent through the booking form of the three tenants on booking-v3 (alder.physio, copper.studio, pale.harbour). The form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field, so people type the month first, and the form then reads the date the tenant’s way, day first. The reports look like the parser’s, but the cause is the hint. A parser fix does not touch it.

3 · Dates move by one day · #3

This problem holds 5 reports, and they name no swapped month. The date moves by one day: “Invite says Tuesday 12 May. Calendar says Monday 11 May”, “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday”, “Every booking in June is one day off”, “The invite arrived before I made the booking”. That is a time-zone or day-boundary bug. The desk noted these reports as one-day moves, but the old cause table still files them under the parser by tenant.

Why they ended up in one ticket

  • Decision 0001 merges on the subject line. “Date is wrong” reads the same for all three problems, so all three landed in #4471.
  • The intake tag is a guess from the reporter’s browser. It names the wrong problem for some reports in every row of the table above, so it cannot split the ticket either.
  • The split here uses what the desk knows about the tenant (locale and booking form), the channel, and the desk’s own notes on one-day moves.

What to do

  • Track each problem in its own ticket: #1, #2, #3.
  • Change the status of #4471 from known to split, and point it to this page.
  • Before the next merge on subject, check the tenant and the channel. Two reports with the same subject can have different causes: several subjects, “Date is wrong” among them, occur in all three problems.
